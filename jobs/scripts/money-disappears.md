content, the narration felt slow and disconnected, the 3D scenes go, 2D only, polished and clear, every picture shows the idea of
its line, transitions and animation planned from the start and flowing into each other.

## Spine
- Hook: every coin, note and account number gone tonight → sounds like everything is free → no pure barter economy, money
  rebuilt within weeks → the promise: what money does, who decides without it, when it was tried, the answer.
- 1) What money does: go-between, measuring stick, a message → without prices the messages go → who gets the last loaf?
- 2) Who gets it: the queue (pay with time, favours spare hours) · rationing (Britain 1940–1954) · whoever writes the card.
- 3) It has been tried: the Inca (mit'a, qullqa, khipu, the state decides) · WWII camps (cigarettes became money, Radford) · money
  keeps coming back (cattle, cowries, coins, paper).
- 4) What people say: back to barter → Humphrey 1985 · nobody would work → Finland, Kenya, one US study: it depends.
- 5) What money really is: about 97 % numbers → accepted because everyone accepts it → a shared promise.
- Conclusion: queues and rules decide · money is rebuilt fast · the cost moves → not free, someone still decides → an agreement.

## Transitions (planned, each carries the idea): zoomthru into a window / match a falling tag onto the price tag / morph the quote
mark into the orbit of money / zoomthru into the crate / match the cigarette onto the board / morph the ACCEPTED tick into the
shared banknote / push2 and wipe to move along the argument / dissolve when time passes / fade only into chapter cards.
