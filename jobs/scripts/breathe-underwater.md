# Director's script — Thở được dưới nước (breathe-underwater)

Written FIRST (owner, 4 Nov 2026). The look: IF CREW v3 + comic layer, the see-in-the-dark standard (a real 2D cartoon, every
shot designed, an in-scene camera rig, action on every line). This episode's own world: WATER. Layered 2.5D seas (surface
light, caustics, drifting particles, bubbles that really rise), cut-away bodies (heart, blood, spleen, lungs) drawn as clean
flat diagrams, and gauges that make the invisible gases visible: oxygen = teal dots, carbon dioxide = coral dots. Layout law:
titles 6–24 %, subjects 26–70 %, the caption band 72–81 % stays calm, labels through CT.sign. One vertical 9:16 film, about
7 min, af_heart at --speed 1.0. Theme: ocean. Opening = the title question (no cold open). No quotations, no legend.
Safety: breath-holding is never shown as a technique to copy; hyperventilation is the danger; "never alone" is said out loud.

REAL: Stéphane Mifsud, Vitomir Maričić, Alexey Molchanov, Bajau, Netherlands, Leiden

## Spine
- Hook: the question over a person in the sea taking a breath of water → 1961, Leiden: a mouse breathed liquid, and what
  stopped it was not a lack of oxygen → the promise: how far humans go on one breath, what the body does underwater, why gills
  alone would not save you.
- 1) One breath: untrained 30–90 s (3 min with grit) → Mifsud 11:35 on air (2009) → Maričić 29:03 with pure oxygen first (2025,
  a separate record) → lung packing → Molchanov 133 m (2024) → same lungs, 30 s or 11 min: the difference is in the body.
- 2) The dive reflex: cold water on the face → heart to 10–15 bpm → blood to brain and heart → the spleen's reserve → the
  Bajau's 50 % larger spleens (genes) → the urge to breathe is a CO2 alarm, the brain cannot read oxygen.
- 3) Give yourself gills: water holds ~30× less oxygen → 800× denser, 30× more to pump → Kylstra's mouse: oxygen was fine,
  CO2 could not get out → 1989 liquid ventilation, still experimental → the real enemy is CO2, and CO2 is what keeps divers alive.
- 4) The deadly trick: hyperventilation adds almost no oxygen (blood already 97–99 % full) → it silences the CO2 alarm →
  shallow water blackout, the leading killer, often near the surface → never alone.
- What people say: "I just need more oxygen" → you already carry almost all you can; the alarm runs on CO2.
- Conclusion: 11 minutes on air · the dive reflex we share with whales and seals · gills would not fix CO2 → breathing underwater
  is not an oxygen problem, it is a carbon dioxide problem → the burning feeling is an old alarm doing its job → next: no night.
