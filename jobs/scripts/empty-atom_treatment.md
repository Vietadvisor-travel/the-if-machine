# Treatment — Bên trong nguyên tử có gì (empty-atom) · director: Claude · 17 Nov 2026
(Written after the film as the MODEL treatment for CINEMA.md §1 — the next films write theirs first.)

## 1. Logline
In a dark room in Manchester in 1909, a twenty-year-old student counting flashes of light sees a particle bounce back from gold foil — and
the solid world turns out to be almost entirely empty.

## 2. The feeling
- word: vertigo (the ground under you is mostly nothing)
- the moment it lands: L58–L61, the viewer's own hand pressed on a table, the camera diving into its emptiness and back out.

## 3. Governing image & motif
- image: the dark round field of Marsden's microscope where one green flash appears on the wrong side.
- motif (shape): the CIRCLE — eyepiece → the atom's dotted shell → the stadium bowl → the round of the viewer's eye at the end.
- returns (≥ 4): L1 — the eyepiece opens · L5 — the circle becomes the uncuttable atom · L49 — the atom's shell becomes the stadium · L57 — the palm's dive through a circular window · L69 — the eyepiece closes on the last flash

## 4. Visual metaphors
- idea "a heavy thing threw the particle back": (1) a stone flung into mist comes back → (2) a 15-inch shell bounces off tissue paper → (3) the needle-thin fan of paths flung from one point
- idea "the atom is empty": (1) a fly in a cathedral → (2) a pea on the pitch of an empty stadium, dust on the top seats → (3) your own hand, almost all empty space

## 5. Set pieces
### Set piece 1 — The flash in the dark (act I, ≈ L0–L5)
- idea: we live one night of counting with Marsden until the impossible happens
- staging: the Manchester lab built in three planes — dark shelves far, the bench and apparatus, a lamp and dust near; Marsden seated, eye at the glass
- camera: wide establishing → slow push to a close on his face → into the eyepiece (POV) → hold on the dark field
- transformation: the eyepiece's circle opens into the zinc-sulphide screen that fills the frame
- sound moment: total quiet, the faint tick of the counter, then one bright 'ting' for the flash on the wrong side
- reveal: the trace runs BACK from the flash to the gold leaf — something threw it back
### Set piece 2 — The shell and the tissue paper (act II, ≈ L42–L47)
- idea: Rutherford's own words made into a picture of the impossible
- staging: a naval gun on a beach, a sheet of tissue paper on a frame, the sea behind (deep set, haze)
- camera: low wide on the gun → whip along the shell's flight → hard stop at the paper → the shell turning back toward us
- transformation: the shell's path becomes the alpha's path round the nucleus; the fan of paths is computed from Coulomb's law
- sound moment: the gun's boom, a held silence, the wrong-way whistle
- reveal: the 1911 nucleus — a point of gold in an empty dotted circle
### Set piece 3 — Your hand is empty (act III, ≈ L57–L63)
- idea: bring the discovery home to the viewer's own body
- staging: a hand pressed on a wooden table in warm light
- camera: insert on the palm → dive through the skin into the atom's emptiness → the electron cushion → back out to the hand
- transformation: skin → lattice of atoms → one atom's emptiness → the cushion of electrons pushing back
- sound moment: the music drops out inside the atom; one soft heartbeat
- reveal: the diamond and the gold ingot, "mostly empty space" — and the hand still solid on the table

## 6. Hero & acting
- hero: Ernest Marsden, 20, a student (with Hans Geiger; Rutherford the mentor)
- want: to count accurately enough to be trusted by Rutherford
- beats (3): tired routine (headhands between counts) → doubt (sitpeer, focus, a second look) → astonishment (sitstartle, awe)
- close-ups: his eye at the glass (L1), his hand on the fine-adjust knob (L2), his face at the flash (L3), Rutherford's grin at the shell (L43)

## 7. Colour script
- act I: cold sepia-indigo night; the only colour is the green of the scintillations
- act II: warm lamp-amber of the Manchester lab; the beach of the shell in pale daylight
- act III: luminous gold on cream — the nucleus, the stadium at dusk, the warm hand
- accent colour: vermilion ink — only on the flash that came back, the ringed nucleus, the one sentence "1 in 8,000"

## 8. Light script
- places & their light: Manchester lab — one green-shaded lamp over the bench, darkness around; the moor — overcast; the stadium — dusk floodlights; the table — warm window light from the left
- the light that tells the story: the lamp is turned down so the eye can see the faint flashes — darkness is the instrument

## 9. Rhythm
- tension curve: slow, quiet build (0–1 min) → curiosity (clues 1–2) → rising tension to the shell (5–6 min) → release into wonder → intimate close
- held breaths (3): the first flash on the wrong side (L3) · the shell coming back (L43) · inside the hand's emptiness (L60)
- accelerations: the montage of Geiger and Marsden counting angle by angle (L47), months in seconds

## 10. Bookends & material
- opening image: the eyepiece opening on a dark field where one flash appears
- closing image (rhymes with it because…): the eyepiece closing on one last flash — but now we know the flash is a messenger from the emptiness inside everything
- real engravings / documents / footage and how they are used: the 1909 and 1911 papers (Phil. Mag., Proc. R. Soc.) pinned as artefacts with the sentence circled; the 1913 table of counts on the curve; cloud-chamber footage in the eyepiece; public-domain physics-textbook plates (tools/etch.py) for the apparatus and the period lab furniture
