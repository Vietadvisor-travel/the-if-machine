## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- 76 days adrift (5 Feb to 21 Apr 1982) (S1, S2).
- 6.5 m: Napoleon Solo, a sloop he designed and built himself (S1).
- About 6 ft across: the Avon inflatable raft (S1). The WEEK file calls it "five-foot"; Wikipedia says about six feet. Use "about six feet".
- Just over 1 pint of fresh water a day from the stills plus rainwater (S1).
- 1,800 nautical miles (3,300 km): the drift (S1).
- 9 ships passed without seeing him (S1).
- A third of his body weight lost (S1).
- 36 weeks on the New York Times bestseller list (S1).
- 3 solar stills aboard; 1 cut apart to learn how it worked (S1).

## Visual notes (place, era, weather/light, clothing, objects, vehicles, what the room/landscape looked like)

- 1982, open Atlantic: endless blue water, trade-wind sky, hard sun, night stars.
- Napoleon Solo: small white 6.5 m sloop, single mast, home-built look.
- The raft: orange Avon inflatable, about six feet across, a man curled inside it, a small canopy of gear, solar stills (clear plastic cones), a short speargun, fishing lines, drying fish strips.
- Callahan: 30, bearded, sunburnt, saltwater sores, thin by the end, a cap against the sun.
- The ecosystem: dorado (mahi-mahi) under the raft, flying fish, birds circling above.
- Rescue: small fishing boat off Marie-Galante, fishermen leaning over the side, birds hovering over the raft.

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- No deaths. Callahan is alive; the story is his own account in his memoir. Keep the "whale" as his guess, never as fact.
- The raft puncture and patch: his own account, 33 days of re-inflating. Do not exaggerate into a second sinking.
- End on the rescue and the life raft he later designed, not on the suffering.
