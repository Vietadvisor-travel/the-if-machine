## Hook line (verb first, mid-action, ≤ 20 words)

He eats bicycles, televisions and shopping carts, then spends two years eating an aeroplane.

## The turn (what the viewer does not expect)

Guinness gives him a brass plaque for the record, and he eats that too.

## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- 900 g to 1 kg of material a day (S1).
- 15 days: first bicycle, 17 Mar - 2 Apr 1977 (S1).
- 12 days: second bicycle, 6.8 kg, plus 100 razor blades, two plates, a glass (S1).
- About 1,100 kg: the Cessna 150, eaten 1978-1980, reportedly (S1, S2). The "entire plane" claim is unverified (Snopes 2022).
- Over 9 tons of metal in his lifetime, including the brass plaque (S1).
- 18 bicycles, 15 trolleys, 7 TV sets, 6 chandeliers, 2 beds, 1 pair of skis, 1 computer, 1 coffin by 1999 (S1).
- 57: age at death, 25 Jun 2007, natural causes (S1, S2).
- $1,000 a day: his fee as a performer, per the Madison Courier 1980 (S1).

## Quotes (exact words only, with who said them and the source; no invented dialogue)

- "I accidentally broke a glass while drinking, and I had a piece in my mouth. I knew other people had eaten glass in the past and decided that I could do it as well. Then I moved on to razor blades, plates and small pieces of metal such as nuts and bolts." - Michel Lotito, quoted in S1.
- "People started to ask me if I wanted to eat something bigger and so I said, 'OK, I think I can eat a bicycle.' It was a great success - they called me for TV shows in South America and Canada." - Michel Lotito, quoted in S1.
- "I would always ask for a doctor's assistance. They would give me all sorts of advice, such as to eat artichokes or paraffin oils together with metal to make things easier, but I soon realized that I was better off simply drinking several litres of water." - Michel Lotito, quoted in S1.
- "My teeth are incredibly strong - their strength has been measured at eight tonnes per centimetre squared. But I also secrete strong juices that cause razor blades to melt in my mouth; my gastric juices are so powerful that, during an endoscopy, doctors observed juices attacking objects with a corrosive foam that ate the metal." - Michel Lotito, quoted in S1. Note: this is his own claim about his body, not a measured medical fact.
- "We've never found a valid medical explanation - biopsies and blood tests have shown that his organism is just like anybody else's." - Dr Morzol, his lifelong doctor, quoted in S1.
- "Aggressing his stomach and intestines like this inevitably raises the question of survival, but we don't have an answer. He's a prototype; we don't know what will happen." - Dr Morzol, quoted in S1.

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- Pica is a real eating disorder. The script must say plainly: do not try this, it can kill you. Never present it as a trick to copy, never mock him.
- The full-plane claim is unverified (Snopes 2022). Say "reportedly" and "he claimed". The Guinness record itself is real; the plaque story is real.
- His doctor's words are the honest counterpoint: no valid medical explanation was ever found. Use them.
