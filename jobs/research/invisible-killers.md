# Research pack — invisible-killers · "How did we discover that invisible germs kill?" · FATHOM · Claude, 17 Nov 2026

## The question
For thousands of years people died of fevers, plagues and infections no one could see coming. Doctors blamed bad air, bad blood, God. How did we discover the real killers — living things far too small to see?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Vienna General Hospital, 1840s: two maternity clinics side by side. In the First (taught medical students, who also did autopsies) about 10 % of mothers died of childbed fever; in the Second (midwives) under 4 % | S1, S2, S3 |
| F2 | 1847: Semmelweis's friend Jakob Kolletschka died after a scalpel cut during an autopsy — with the same signs as childbed fever. Semmelweis concluded "cadaverous particles" on students' hands carried it | S1, S3 |
| F3 | May 1847: handwashing in chlorinated lime. First Clinic deaths fell to about 1–2 % (sources: 1.3 % in 1848; monthly average ≈ 2 %) | S1, S2, S4 |
| F4 | He was not believed; his post was not renewed (1849); he was committed to an asylum and died there in August 1865 | S1, S2 |
| F5 | Pasteur's swan-neck flasks (≈ 1859–62): boiled broth in a flask whose neck bent like a swan's stayed clear — air could get in, dust and microbes could not; break the neck and it spoiled in days | S5, S6, S7 |
| F6 | Koch (1876): the anthrax bacillus grown alone, injected, caused anthrax — one microbe, one disease; 1882: the tuberculosis bacillus, photographed through the microscope | S5, S6, S7 |

## Clues
1. **Two clinics, two death rates** (F1). 2. **A scalpel and a friend's death** (F2, F3). 3. **The flask that stayed clean** (F5). 4. **Photographing the killer** (F6).
Twist: the man who saved mothers by washing hands was laughed at and died in an asylum — the same year the world began to listen (Lister's antiseptic surgery, 1865).
