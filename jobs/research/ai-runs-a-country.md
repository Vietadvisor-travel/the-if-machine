# ai-runs-a-country — research pack (Muse)

- Date: 2026-11-03 · Score: 87 (37/17/16/17) · Type: trend

## Question (one line, as the viewer would ask it)

What if an AI ran a country for one year?

## Twist (the surprising answer, one or two lines)

The machine would not win by being smarter than humans. It would win by being boringly consistent about small decisions, and the first crisis would not be a robot takeover, it would be one citizen asking who to appeal to when the machine is wrong.

## Canvas beats (what each beat draws, in order; the picture is the SUBJECT of the line)
1. A lever pulls, a ticket prints the question, a map of a small generic country lights up, no flag, no real leader.
2. A citizen opens a phone, a tax form fills itself in seconds, a queue of paper forms shrinks to nothing.
3. A hospital queue, a school timetable and a bus map all update at once on one dashboard.
4. A welfare application turns red, a citizen taps appeal, the appeal goes to a human inbox, not to another machine.
5. A budget bar chart rebalances itself, roads get a little more, a bridge project waits a year.
6. A protest sign outside a plain government building, the sign reads only: who decides the appeal.
7. The sim clock jumps to day 365, the ticket prints the answer: faster, fairer on small things, still human on big ones.

## Notes / traps (numbers that are easy to get wrong, claims to avoid)

- Keep the country generic. Do not name a real country as the AI-run one, do not use a real leader's face, voice or name.
- Do not claim Estonia is run by AI. Estonia is the closest real anchor, with digital services and AI projects inside a human government.
- Do not present Robodebt as an AI model failure only. It was an automated data matching system, and the lesson is about appeal and oversight, not about model size.
- Label every prediction about year one as a guess on screen: "Here's where the machine has to guess."
- The 99 percent and 100 percent Estonia figures come from different sources. Use "about 99 percent" in narration, never both in one sentence.
