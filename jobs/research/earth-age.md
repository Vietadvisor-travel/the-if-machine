## The question
Nobody was there to write it down. For centuries the age of the Earth was guessed from scripture, then from erosion, cooling and the sea's
salt — and every answer was wrong. How did we finally read the planet's real age, and what did the man who measured it discover on the way?

## Facts (S = sources below)
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | 1650: Archbishop James Ussher, *Annales Veteris Testamenti*: creation began at nightfall before 23 October 4004 BC (counted back through biblical generations; later printed in the margins of English Bibles) | S1, S2, S3 |
| F2 | 1859: Darwin, *Origin of Species* (1st ed.), estimated the erosion of the Weald (SE England) at about 300 million years — he dropped it from later editions after criticism | S4, S5 |
| F3 | 1862/64: William Thomson (Lord Kelvin) treated the Earth as a cooling ball: best figure ~100 million years, range 20–400 million; by 1897 he narrowed it to 20–40 million | S6, S7, S8 |
| F4 | 1899: John Joly used the salt in the oceans as a clock (salt in ÷ salt per year): about 80–90 million years — flawed, salt also leaves the sea | S5, S9 |
| F5 | 1904: Ernest Rutherford, Royal Institution lecture with Kelvin in the audience: radioactivity is a new source of heat inside the Earth — and its steady decay is a clock | S6, S10 |
| F6 | 1907: Bertram Boltwood (Yale) published the first uranium–lead ages of minerals: about 410 to 2,200 million years | S11, S12 |
| F7 | 1948: Clair Patterson (with George Tilton, Chicago) set out to measure lead isotopes in old rock; his samples were contaminated by lead from everywhere, so he built one of the first ultra-clean laboratories (at Caltech) | S13, S14 |
| F8 | 1953: lead from the Canyon Diablo iron meteorite (Arizona) measured on a mass spectrometer at Argonne; 1956 paper "Age of meteorites and the earth": 4.55 ± 0.07 billion years — still the accepted figure (~4.54) | S13, S14, S15 |
| F9 | His clean-lab work showed modern lead everywhere (air, water, people): Greenland snow/ice lead rose about 200-fold since antiquity, mostly from leaded petrol (Rosman et al. 1993) | S16, S17 |
| F10 | Patterson campaigned against leaded petrol (opposed by the lead industry); US phase-down from the 1970s, banned for on-road vehicles from 1 January 1996 | S17, S18 |

## Clues (story order)
1. **Counting people** — Ussher's generations (F1). 2. **Counting stone and heat** — Darwin's erosion vs Kelvin's cooling (F2, F3, F4).
3. **The clock inside rocks** — Rutherford, Boltwood (F5, F6). 4. **A meteorite and a clean room** — Patterson (F7, F8).
Twist: to read the clock he had to clean the world's lead out of his lab — and found it in us; he ended leaded petrol (F9, F10).
