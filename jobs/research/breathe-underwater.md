## Question (one line, as the viewer would ask it)

What if you could breathe underwater?

## The answer (what the evidence actually says, one or two lines; objective, no storyline)

Gills would not save you. The real limit is not oxygen, it is carbon dioxide, cold and pressure: your urge to breathe comes from CO2 buildup, not from running out of air.

## Entertaining angles (the facts that make people say "really?": surprising numbers, comparisons, history, records)
- 29 minutes on a single breath, and the trick is breathing pure oxygen for half an hour first.
- Your heart can slow to 10 beats a minute just because your face got wet and cold.
- Breathing fast before a dive does not give you more oxygen, it deletes your warning system, and that is what kills freedivers.
- An untrained person lasts 30 to 90 seconds; a trained one lasts 11 minutes on the same lungs.

## Notes / traps (numbers that are easy to get wrong, claims to avoid)
- Keep the two breath-hold records apart on screen: 11:35 is the no-oxygen AIDA record (Mifsud, 2009); 24:37 and 29:03 are Guinness oxygen-assisted records (Šobat 2021, Maričić 2025). Do not present 29:03 as a plain-air record.
- The "12 cubic metres of seawater per second" estimate from the shortlist is from a weak source: use it only as "about", or drop it and lean on the CO2 facts instead.
- The gill-efficiency figure (~75% extraction) is from a weak source too; keep the gills point qualitative (water carries far less oxygen than air) unless a stronger source is found.
- Never present hyperventilation as a technique: it is the danger. The episode must not read as freediving instruction; add the "never dive alone" rule.
