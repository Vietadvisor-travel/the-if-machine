## Question (one line, as the viewer would ask it)

What if you could see in the dark?

## The answer (what the evidence actually says, one or two lines; objective, no storyline)

True night vision is not brighter eyes, it is seeing heat. The world would turn into a thermal map, and color would be gone.

## Entertaining angles (the facts that make people say "really?": surprising numbers, comparisons, history, records)

- A cat's eye mirror reflects 130 times more light than the back of your eye, which is why cat eyes glow in photos.
- The snake's heat sense runs on the same receptor that makes wasabi burn: TRPA1, retuned for heat.

## Notes / traps (numbers that are easy to get wrong, claims to avoid)
- Cat night vision still needs some light: it is NOT seeing in total darkness (F10). Say so on screen.
- "Six times more rods" is a density figure, not a total count: cats and humans have similar total rod numbers (~120 million); cats pack them denser. Do not say cats have six times as many rods in total.
- The 6.2x contrast figure is from one behavioural study (Kang & Malpeli 2009); the popular "6x better" claim traces to it.
- Pit viper detection distance is about 1 metre under ideal conditions, less in rain or humidity.

## Addendum (Claude, 11 Nov 2026, production): facts checked and added
- F5/F6 reworded: the pit membrane responds to temperature changes of "a few thousandths of a degree" (0.003 °C: Bullock & Diecke 1956, via the pack's sources; 0.001 °C in other secondary sources). The "ten times better than military cameras" line is NOT used; instead: a cooled thermal camera resolves about 0.02 °C (FLIR, "What You Need to Know About IR Detectors", May 2025, https://magazine.flir.com/innovate-and-inspire/issue5-may2025/what-you-need-to-know-about-ir-detectors).
- F11 (NEW): the snake's heat sensor is TRPA1, the "wasabi receptor", the most heat-sensitive vertebrate ion channel known (Gracheva et al., Nature 2010, https://www.nature.com/articles/nature08943 · PMC2855400); pit signals reach the optic tectum, where they are combined with vision (Newman & Hartline 1981, cited there).
- F12 (NEW): humans can detect a single photon slightly better than chance (51.6 % correct in a two-interval test; Tinsley et al., Nature Communications 2016, doi 10.1038/ncomms12172 · Rockefeller University news, https://www.rockefeller.edu/news/11808-study-suggests-humans-can-detect-even-the-smallest-units-of-light/).
- F13 (NEW): 2019, mice injected with upconversion nanoparticles saw near-infrared (980 nm → green ~550 nm), told infrared shapes apart, daylight vision unaffected (Ma et al., Cell 2019, doi 10.1016/j.cell.2019.01.038 · The Scientist, https://the-scientist.com/news-opinion/nanoparticles-let-mice-see-near-infrared-light-65545). Duration is reported differently (2 vs 10 weeks): not stated in the film.
- F14 (NEW): the carrot story spread in WWII when Britain's Ministry of Information credited night pilots' carrots while airborne radar stayed secret; vitamin A only restores normal vision (We Are The Mighty, https://wearethemighty.com/popular/myth-about-carrots-and-good-eyesight · Science Norway, https://www.sciencenorway.no/culture-food-and-health-war/where-the-carrot-myth-comes-from/2665915). Whether it fooled anyone is disputed: not claimed.
- F15 (NEW): image-intensifier goggles turn photons into electrons and multiply them (up to ~50,000×) onto a green phosphor screen; they need some ambient light (Wikipedia "Image intensifier", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_intensifier · AGM Global Vision, https://www.agmglobalvision.eu/blog/why-is-night-vision-green). Said as "tens of thousands of times".
- F16 (NEW): ordinary glass is opaque to thermal (long-wave) infrared: thermal cameras cannot see through windows and use germanium lenses (Infiniti Optics, https://www.infinitioptics.com/technology/thermal-imaging).
- F17 (NEW): a cat's slit pupil changes its area about 135-fold, a human's about 15-fold (Banks et al., "Why do animal eyes have pupils of different shapes?", Science Advances 2015, doi 10.1126/sciadv.1500391).
- F18 (reasoning, said as such): a warm eye would be flooded by its own heat glow; the snake's pit membrane hangs in an air-filled chamber and many thermal cameras cool their sensors (FLIR, above: cryocooled detectors).
- F19 (NEW): colour fades at night because colour comes from three kinds of cones, which need bright light; in dim light the rods take over and see only shades of grey (Live Science, "Why can't we see colors well in the dark?", https://www.livescience.com/health/neuroscience/why-cant-we-see-colors-well-in-the-dark · HyperPhysics, Rods and Cones, https://hyperphysics.gsu.edu/hbase/vision/rodcone.html).
- F20 (NEW): the centre of the eye (fovea) has almost no rods, so a faint star can vanish when looked at directly and reappear with averted vision, looking slightly to the side (Astronomy magazine, June 2007, https://www.astronomy.com/science/bob-bermans-strange-universe-dont-look-at-me-like-that/ · Wikipedia, Averted vision). A 2021 study found the best angle closer (~8°) than rod density alone predicts: not stated as a number.
- F21 (NEW, from F12's paper): a photon that arrived a few seconds earlier made the next detection more likely (priming; Tinsley et al. 2016).
- F22 (NEW, from F11's paper): pit-viper TRPA1 is the most heat-sensitive vertebrate ion channel identified (Gracheva et al., Nature 2010).
