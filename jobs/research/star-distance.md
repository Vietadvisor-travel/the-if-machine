# Research pack — star-distance · "How far away are the stars?" · FATHOM · Claude, 15 Nov 2026

## The question
A star is a point of light with no size and no label. For two thousand years astronomers knew the stars must be far — but how far, nobody
could measure. How did we put a number on the distance to the stars, and then to other galaxies?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Parallax: a near star shifts against far ones as the Earth moves around the Sun; the shift is tiny — under one arcsecond for every star | S1, S2 |
| F2 | 1838: Friedrich Bessel (Königsberg) measured 61 Cygni's parallax: 0.314″ (modern 0.286″) → about 10 light-years (modern ~11.4) — the first reliable star distance | S1, S2, S3 |
| F3 | Almost at the same time: Thomas Henderson (Alpha Centauri, observed at the Cape 1832–33, published 1839) and Friedrich Struve (Vega) | S2, S4 |
| F4 | 0.314″ is the angle of a 2-cm coin seen from about 13 km (calculation from F2) | calc |
| F5 | Parallax fails for most stars: too far. Harvard College Observatory employed women to measure photographic plates; Henrietta Swan Leavitt studied variable stars on plates of the Magellanic Clouds taken at Arequipa, Peru | S5, S6 |
| F6 | 1912 (Harvard College Observatory Circular, signed by Pickering, "prepared by Miss Leavitt"): 25 Cepheids in the Small Magellanic Cloud — the longer the period, the brighter the star → a "standard candle" | S5, S6, S7 |
| F7 | 26 April 1920, the "Great Debate" (Smithsonian): Shapley vs Curtis — are the spiral nebulae inside our galaxy or other galaxies? No winner | S8, S9 |
| F8 | Night of 5–6 Oct 1923, Mount Wilson 100-inch Hooker telescope: Edwin Hubble's plate H335H of Andromeda; he crossed out "N" (nova) and wrote "VAR!" — a Cepheid (M31-V1) → Andromeda lies far outside the Milky Way | S8, S10, S11 |
| F9 | 1927 Lemaître, 1929 Hubble: the farther a galaxy, the faster it recedes → the universe is expanding | S12, S13 |
| F10 | Today the Gaia space telescope has measured parallax distances for about 1.5 billion stars (EDR3: 1.47 billion) | S14 |

## Clues
1. **The wobble** — parallax and Bessel's 61 Cygni (F1–F4). 2. **The blinking stars** — Leavitt's Cepheids (F5, F6).
3. **One letter on a glass plate** — Hubble's "VAR!" (F7, F8). 4. **The runaway galaxies** — expansion (F9).
Twist: measuring how far the stars are revealed that the universe is getting bigger — and today a telescope measures a billion stars the way Bessel measured one (F10).
