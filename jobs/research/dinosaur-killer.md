# Research pack — dinosaur-killer · "How do we know what killed the dinosaurs?" · FATHOM · Claude, 17 Nov 2026

## The question
Sixty-six million years ago the dinosaurs vanished, with most life on Earth. No one saw it. How did we work out what happened on the last day of their world?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | At Gubbio, Italy, a thin layer of clay (about a centimetre) separates rock full of Cretaceous microfossils from rock almost empty of them — the K–Pg boundary | S1, S2 |
| F2 | Walter Alvarez (geologist) and his father Luis (Nobel physicist) measured iridium in the clay to time how fast it settled — and found far too much (sources: ≈ 30× to 160× background) | S1, S2, S3 |
| F3 | 1980, Science 208: Alvarez, Alvarez, Asaro, Michel: the iridium came from an asteroid about 10 km across | S2, S3 |
| F4 | The same iridium layer was found around the world; then shocked quartz — crystals scarred by pressures only an impact makes | S2, S3 |
| F5 | 1978: Pemex geophysicists Glen Penfield and Antonio Camargo, surveying for oil, found a buried ring on the Yucatán coast; presented in 1981 with little notice | S4, S5 |
| F6 | 1990–91: Alan Hildebrand traced the clues to the Yucatán; old Pemex drill cores showed shock-metamorphic minerals; 1991 paper named it Chicxulub (≈ 180 km wide) | S4, S5, S6 |
| F7 | The crater is named after the village of Chicxulub Pueblo; its centre lies near the coast, partly offshore | S5, S6 |
| F8 | The extinction is dated to about 66 million years ago (the K–Pg boundary) | S2, S6 |
| F9 | About three-quarters of plant and animal species disappeared, including all non-bird dinosaurs | S2, S6 |
| F10 | Dust, soot and sulphate aerosols from the impact darkened and cooled the Earth for years ("impact winter"); photosynthesis collapsed and food chains with it | S2, S6 |
| F11 | Small mammals survived; birds are the surviving line of dinosaurs | S2, S6 |

(F8–F11 added at production 18 Nov 2026 from the pack's own sources S2 and S6; the session had no web access to re-read them, so the film states them without figures beyond "about three-quarters".)

## Clues
1. **The line in the rock** (F1). 2. **Too much of a rare metal** (F2, F3). 3. **Scars in the sand** (F4). 4. **The ring under the jungle** (F5–F7).
Twist: the "smoking gun" crater had been found a decade earlier by oil prospectors — and sat in a company report.
