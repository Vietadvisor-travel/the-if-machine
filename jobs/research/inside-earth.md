# Research pack — inside-earth · "What is inside the Earth?" · FATHOM · Claude, 15 Nov 2026

## The question
The centre of the Earth is about 6,371 km below our feet. The deepest hole ever drilled reached barely 12 km. Nobody has ever seen what is
down there — so how do we know there is a solid metal ball at the centre, as hot as the surface of the Sun?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Kola Superdeep Borehole (USSR, Arctic): 12,262 m in 1989 — the deepest hole by vertical depth; work stopped 1992–95, heat higher than expected. ≈ 0.2 % of the way to the centre | S1, S2 |
| F2 | 1798: Henry Cavendish's torsion balance gave the Earth's density as 5.48 × water (his arithmetic slip; 5.45 correct) — about twice the rock we stand on, so the inside must be far heavier | S3, S4 |
| F3 | 1890s: Emil Wiechert proposed an iron core, like iron meteorites | S4, S5 |
| F4 | Earthquakes send waves through the planet: fast P (push) and slower S (shake) waves; seismographs worldwide record them | S6, S7 |
| F5 | 1906: Richard Dixon Oldham (Geological Survey of India), from the 1897 Assam earthquake records: waves crossing the far side arrive late → a core | S8, S9 |
| F6 | 1909: Andrija Mohorovičić, Zagreb, from the 8 Oct 1909 Kulpa-valley (Pokuplje) earthquake: a boundary tens of km down where waves speed up — the crust/mantle "Moho" | S9, S10 |
| F7 | 1914: Beno Gutenberg put the core boundary at about 2,900 km deep; S waves do not cross the outer core → it is liquid | S11, S12 |
| F8 | A shadow zone ~103°–143° from a quake where direct P waves should not arrive — yet faint ones did | S11, S12 |
| F9 | 1936: Inge Lehmann (Copenhagen), studying records of the 1929 Buller (Murchison) earthquake, New Zealand, explained them by a solid inner core; her paper's title was just "P′" | S11, S12, S13 |
| F10 | Inner core: radius about 1,220 km, solid iron–nickel, about 5,400 °C — similar to the Sun's surface; liquid outer core motion makes the magnetic field. Lehmann lived to 104 | S14, S15, S11 |

## Clues
1. **The weight** — Cavendish: the Earth is too heavy to be rock all the way down (F2, F3). 2. **The echo** — earthquake waves as X-rays; Oldham's late waves (F4, F5).
3. **The liquid** — the core boundary, S waves vanish (F6, F7). 4. **The ghost wave** — Lehmann's P′ in the shadow zone (F8, F9).
Twist: the deepest place we know best is one no one will ever reach — mapped by a woman reading squiggles on paper (F1, F10).
