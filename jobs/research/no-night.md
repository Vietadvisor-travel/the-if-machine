## Question (one line, as the viewer would ask it)

What if there was never any night?

## The answer (what the evidence actually says, one or two lines; objective, no storyline)

No night does not mean endless summer. It means one side of the planet bakes for months while the other freezes, and every clock inside your body breaks.

## Entertaining angles (the facts that make people say "really?": surprising numbers, comparisons, history, records)
- A real planet has a year shorter than a work shift: 5.4 hours, with a dayside at 2,524 K.
- On Mercury, one day is twice as long as one year.
- In Longyearbyen, people live four months without a sunset, then four months without a sunrise, and hold a festival when the sun comes back.
- A plant can be stopped from flowering by a few minutes of red light in the middle of the night.

## Notes / traps (numbers that are easy to get wrong, claims to avoid)
- Overlap risk: the physics overlaps with candidate 1 (Earth stops spinning). Do NOT pick both in the same week. Owner picked this one, not candidate 1. On screen, keep the framing on "no night" (tidal locking / permanent day side), not on "Earth stops spinning".
- TOI-2431b's 2524 K is the dayside brightness temperature from the joint TESS+Hubble fit assuming negligible reflected light; with albedo 0.1 it drops to ~2407 K. Do not present it as a surface thermometer reading.
- Mercury's 176 days is the solar day (sunrise to sunrise); its rotation period is 58.6 days. Do not mix the two.
- Svalbard dates: midnight sun 20 Apr-23 Aug and polar night 26 Oct-15 Feb are for Longyearbyen; the "civil polar night" (darkest part) is 14 Nov-29 Jan. Keep the labels straight.
- F4 (stopped Earth) is from a weak source (Little Astronomy); the mechanism is corroborated by the Mercury and tidal-locking facts, but do not add numbers beyond "about a year / about six months".
