## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- Depth: 30 m (Guardian; BBC). One book review gives 34 m, but the two news sources agree on 30 m, so use 30 m.
- Distance offshore: about 20 miles (32 km) (Guardian; BBC).
- Crew: 12, 11 died, 1 survived (Guardian).
- Tug length: 31.5 m (The Diver and the Cook, via Dive Magazine).
- Air pocket: about 1.2 m high, 4 ft square (Guardian; BBC).
- Time in the pocket: almost 60 hours (Guardian headline figure; "three days" is the rounded version).
- Decompression: 3 days (Guardian).
- Capsize time: early morning; Okene recalls "around 5am", reports give about 4:30 a.m. Say "early morning, around 5 a.m." and do not fix it to one minute.

## Quotes (exact words only, with who said them and the source; no invented dialogue)

1. "It was around 5am and I was on the toilet when the vessel just started going down, the speed was so, so fast." Harrison Okene to The Guardian, 12 Jun 2013.
2. "All around me was just black, and noisy. I was crying and calling on Jesus to rescue me, I prayed so hard. I was so hungry and thirsty and cold and I was just praying to see some kind of light." Okene to The Guardian, 12 Jun 2013.
3. "The toilet was now on the ceiling, and I was on the floor, the floor, that just a moment ago had been the ceiling." Okene, quoted in The Diver and the Cook by Lasse Spang Olsen, via Dive Magazine review.
4. "I was there in the water in total darkness just thinking it's the end. I kept thinking the water was going to fill up the room but it did not... I was so hungry but mostly so, so thirsty. The salt water took the skin off my tongue." Okene to Reuters / BBC, 13 Jun 2013.
5. VO only, never staged: "I could perceive the dead bodies of my crew were nearby. I could smell them. The fish came in and began eating the bodies. I could hear the sound. It was horror." Okene, via Gizmodo, 2 Dec 2013.

Do not use until verified: later reports say Okene trained as a commercial diver after the rescue. Neither of the two Guardian sources above verifies it, so do not script it until it is checked against the book or a news follow-up.

## Visual notes (place, era, weather/light, clothing, objects, vehicles, what the room/landscape looked like)

- Night turning to early morning, heavy swells off Nigeria; a 31.5 m tugboat with wheelhouse and open deck, working beside an oil tanker at a Chevron platform.
- Shared bathroom: two large sinks and industrial washing machines and dryers along one wall, toilet booths along the other (book extract). Okene in a toilet booth, wearing only his underpants.
- After the flip: total darkness, the room upside down, water rising slowly, a toilet falling from the ceiling.
- The air pocket: an engineer's cabin, a small platform keeping part of his body above the cold water, a can of cola and sardines.
- The rescue: a saturation diver in a dark wetsuit with a commercial diving helmet and chest lamp, swimming through black water with a torch beam; the diving bell; the white decompression chamber cylinder.
- No daylight until the bell breaks the surface.

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- 11 colleagues died. Tell with respect, never as a joke. The absurdity is the toilet, never the deaths.
- Quote 5 above (fish and bodies) is voice-over only, never staged, never shown in detail. Do not show bodies.
- The clip must say "animated reenactment". The real rescue video is DCN helmet-cam footage; the clip is a reenactment of it, not the footage.
- End on the rescue and survival, not on the deaths.
