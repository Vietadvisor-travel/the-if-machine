# Research pack — ice-ages · "How do we know the Earth was once frozen?" · FATHOM · Claude, 17 Nov 2026

## The question
Today there are palm trees where cities stand on ancient seabed — and great boulders lying in green fields where no river could carry them. How did we discover that huge sheets of ice once covered half the world, and come and go on a schedule?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | Erratic boulders: giant rocks lying far from the mountains they came from (the Jura, northern Europe, America) — long blamed on Noah's flood | S1, S2 |
| F2 | Swiss naturalists (Venetz, de Charpentier) and Karl Schimper (who coined "Eiszeit", ice age, 1837) argued glaciers once reached far beyond today's | S1, S2 |
| F3 | July 1837, Neuchâtel: Louis Agassiz's "discourse" to the Swiss Society of Natural Sciences — a great ice age; the audience was sceptical | S1, S2 |
| F4 | Scratched and polished bedrock, U-shaped valleys and moraines far from any glacier were the evidence | S1, S3 |
| F5 | 1920s–30s: Milutin Milanković calculated how the Earth's orbit and tilt slowly change the sunlight reaching the north — a schedule for ice ages | S1, S3, S4 |
| F6 | Ice cores: air bubbles trapped in Antarctic ice. EPICA Dome C reaches back about 800,000 years; CO₂ in the bubbles rises and falls with temperature through eight glacial cycles (Nature 2008) | S5, S6 |

## Clues
1. **Boulders in the wrong place** (F1). 2. **The ice that once filled the valleys** (F2–F4). 3. **A calendar in the sky** (F5). 4. **Ancient air in the ice** (F6).
Twist: we can breathe — almost literally — the air of 800,000 years ago, trapped in bubbles of Antarctic ice.
