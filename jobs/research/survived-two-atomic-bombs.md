## Hook line (verb first, mid-action, ≤ 20 words)

He survives the first atomic bomb, goes home to tell his boss, and the second bomb falls.

## The turn (what the viewer does not expect)

The second bomb falls at 11:02 a.m., while he is in the office describing the first one.

## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- 29: his age in August 1945 (S1).
- 08:15, 6 Aug 1945: Little Boy over Hiroshima; about 80,000 killed immediately (S1).
- Less than 2 miles (about 3 km): his distance from ground zero at both blasts (S1).
- 11:02, 9 Aug 1945: Fat Man over Nagasaki (S1).
- About 165 people experienced both bombings; 1 officially recognised as a twice-bombed person (S1).
- 24 Mar 2009: certification as nijuu hibakusha (S1).
- 93: age at death, 4 Jan 2010 (S1).
- At least 100,000 people died in the two bombings (S1). Say this once, plainly.

## Quotes (exact words only, with who said them and the source; no invented dialogue)

- "I didn't know what had happened. I think I fainted for a while. When I opened my eyes, everything was dark, and I couldn't see much. It was like the start of a film at the cinema before the picture has begun when the blank frames are just flashing up without any sound." - Tsutomu Yamaguchi, to The Times, quoted in S1.
- "I thought the mushroom cloud had followed me from Hiroshima." - Tsutomu Yamaguchi, to The Independent, quoted in S1.
- "I had no feeling about it. I was neither sorry nor glad. I was seriously ill with a fever, eating almost nothing, hardly even drinking. I thought that I was about to cross to the other side." - Tsutomu Yamaguchi, on the surrender, to The Times, quoted in S1.
- "Having experienced atomic bombings twice and survived, it is my destiny to talk about it." - Tsutomu Yamaguchi, United Nations speech, 2006, quoted in S1. (Note: some retellings give this as "it is my destiny that I must speak out"; use the HISTORY wording.)
- The light "resembling the lightning of a huge magnesium flare" is Yamaguchi's own description of the Hiroshima flash, quoted in S1.

## Visual notes (place, era, weather/light, clothing, objects, vehicles, what the room/landscape looked like)

- 1945, Hiroshima, summer morning: a B-29 overhead, a small object on a parachute, a blinding white flash, a ditch by the road, a potato patch, a mushroom cloud rising, dust blotting out the sun.
- Yamaguchi: 29, Japanese engineer, 1940s work clothes, bandaged hands and face after the first blast, a suit for the office.
- The train: an overnight train full of burned passengers, flickering fires outside, twisted bridges.
- Nagasaki office: 1940s Japanese office, wooden desks, paper, a director's meeting, windows shattering, glass and debris in the air.
- Home: a damaged house, a tunnel shelter, his wife with the baby.
- Do not stage the blasts as spectacle. The flash is white light, then darkness and ash. No bodies on screen; the river crossing is one line of VO at most, never staged.

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- At least 100,000 people died in the two bombings. Never a joke, never "the luckiest man". This is a story about a man who survived twice and spent his old age speaking against the bombs.
- End on his UN speech, not on the bombs. The mushroom-cloud line is his own words and may be used once.
- His wife and son are the human thread; they survived, and the script must say so plainly so the viewer is not left fearing the worst.
