## Hook line (verb first, mid-action, ≤ 20 words)

Five hundred people die in ten days, and the killer is the water pump on their street.

## The turn (what the viewer does not expect)

He draws a dot map, and the dots make a circle around one pump; the handle comes off, and the dying stops.

## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- 127 deaths in the first three days on or near Broad Street (S1, S2).
- "Upwards of five hundred fatal attacks of cholera in ten days" (Snow's own report, quoted in S1). Parish records give 616; a reappraisal gives 578; Wikipedia gives 420 deaths for the Golden Square outbreak itself. The hook uses Snow's "five hundred"; the pack must not present one fixed total as settled.
- 61 instances where the deceased were known to drink Broad Street pump water (Snow's report, S1 and S2).
- 535 workhouse inmates, 5 died (S1).
- Above 70 brewery workmen, none died (S1).
- 3 ft (0.9 m): distance from the well to the leaking cesspit (S2).
- 14 times: cholera mortality in Southwark and Vauxhall homes vs Lambeth homes over 7 weeks (S1, S2). Table: S&V 40,046 houses, 1,263 deaths, 315 per 10,000; Lambeth 26,107 houses, 98 deaths, 37 per 10,000 (S1).
- 7 Sep 1854: the Board meeting; 8 Sep 1854: the handle removed (S1, S2).

## Quotes (exact words only, with who said them and the source; no invented dialogue)

- "On proceeding to the spot, I found that nearly all the deaths had taken place within a short distance of the [Broad Street] pump." - John Snow, On the Mode of Communication of Cholera (1855), quoted in S1 and S2.
- "There were sixty-one instances in which I was informed that the deceased persons used to drink the water from Broad Street, either constantly or occasionally." - John Snow, quoted in S1.
- "I had an interview with the Board of Guardians of St. James parish, on the evening of Thursday, the 7th September, and represented the above circumstances to them. In consequence of what I said, the handle of the pump was removed on the following day." - John Snow, quoted in S1 and S2.
- "There is no doubt that the mortality was much diminished, as I said before, by the flight of the population, which commenced soon after the outbreak; but the attacks had so far diminished before the use of the water was stopped, that it is impossible to decide whether the well still contained the cholera poison in an active state, or whether, from some cause, the water had become free from it." - John Snow, quoted in S2. This is the honest correction the script must carry.
- "The most terrible outbreak of cholera ... took place (in London) in Broad Street, Golden Square, and the adjoining streets, a few weeks ago ... there were upwards of five hundred fatal attacks of cholera in ten days." - John Snow, quoted in S1.

## Visual notes (place, era, weather/light, clothing, objects, vehicles, what the room/landscape looked like)

- 1854, Soho, London: narrow streets, brick terraces, Golden Square, horse carts, a black cast-iron street pump with a long handle at the corner of Broad Street and Cambridge Street.
- Snow: 41, dark coat, top hat, notebook, going door to door with Reverend Whitehead.
- The map: a hand-drawn street plan with black bars stacked at each address, the bars piling highest around the pump.
- The brewery: Broad Street brewery, workmen with beer, copper vats, a deep well of their own.
- The pump handle coming off: a parish workman unscrewing it the morning after the meeting.
- Do not stage cholera deaths graphically; show the map and the pump, not the dying.

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- Hundreds of real people died. Tell it as a detective story about the map, not as a horror story about the dying.
- The honest correction is mandatory: the epidemic was already declining and Snow himself said the handle's role cannot be decided. Never say "he cured cholera" or "the handle ended the epidemic".
- The widow in Hampstead who had Broad Street water carted to her house because she liked the taste, and died of it, is in Snow's own report; use it only as one line of the human thread, with the brewery workers as the counterpoint.
