## Question (one line, as the viewer would ask it)

What if money disappeared and everything was free?

## Twist (the surprising answer, one or two lines)

Free does not remove cost, it moves cost. The price tag goes away, but the queue, the ration card and the person who decides what is scarce take its place, and the first things to run out are not yachts, they are the free things everyone wants at the same time.

## Canvas beats (what each beat draws, in order; the picture is the SUBJECT of the line)
1. A lever pulls, a price tag falls off a loaf of bread, a shop door opens, everyone walks in at once.
2. A wallet empties itself, coins turn to dust, a market stall with no till.
3. A queue forms outside a free phone shop, the queue wraps around the block, a sign flips to: sold out, come back tomorrow.
4. A ration card prints, one phone per household, a family argues over who gets the new one first.
5. A farmer, a baker and a teacher trade hours instead of money, a chalkboard lists who owes whom a morning of work.
6. A hospital, a school and a power station keep running, a dispatcher, not a price, decides what gets fuel first.
7. The sim clock hits day 365, the ticket prints the answer: free food, but not free choice.

## Notes / traps (numbers that are easy to get wrong, claims to avoid)

- Do not claim cash trials prove people stop working, or prove they work more. Finland, Kenya and the US point in different directions, and none of them tested a whole country with no money at all.
- Do not confuse basic income with no money. Basic income still uses money. This episode removes money itself, so the rationing story is the point.
- The Inca point is about state provision, not about a modern free market. Keep it to one beat, do not turn it into a full history lesson.
- Label the year-one outcomes as guesses on screen. The verified facts are the history and the trials, not the future.
- Coin dates vary by a few decades across sources. Use "around 630 BCE, in Lydia" and do not pin it to a single year.
