# Platforms in 2026: lengths, safe zones, posting times, AI labels

Date: 3 Nov 2026. Written by Muse for the engine phase (jobs/tasks/MUSE_ENGINE_PHASE.md,
task 4). Every fact below carries a source and a date. Where sources disagree, the
disagreement is stated, not smoothed over. This file is about the rules of the platforms;
BRAND.md still decides what we publish. Where a platform rule conflicts with a BRAND
default, the conflict is flagged in section 6 and the platform rule wins for that surface
only.

Audience: curious English-speaking adults, mainly US, UK, CA, AU (BRAND section 1).
All posting times below are therefore given in US Eastern Time first, with the UK
conversion. In November the US is on Eastern Standard Time (UTC-5) and the UK is on
Greenwich Mean Time (UTC+0), so UK time is Eastern plus 5 hours. Replace every generic
window below with our own analytics after two weeks of real posts; the platforms' own
"when your audience is online" screens outrank any guide.

## 1. Length: what each platform accepts versus what actually works

### YouTube long-form (our hub)
- Our format: 5-6 minutes, 16:9, per BRAND section 6. That sits at the short end of the
  educational explainer band (guides put explainers at 7-15 minutes, long documentaries
  at 20-40), and it sits between xkcd's What If? (3:39 average) and Half as Interesting
  (about 7 minutes). Length is not a ranking factor; the shortest runtime that fully
  delivers the title's promise wins.
- Monetization note, not a reason to pad: videos under 8 minutes get pre-roll and
  post-roll ads only, no mid-rolls. Padding a 5 minute answer to 8 minutes to chase a
  mid-roll would break the promise and the retention. Ship the answer at its natural
  length.
- Shorts RPM warning (AIR Media-Tech, September 2026, 274 channels): Shorts RPM runs
  3% to 14% of long-form RPM in almost every niche, so roughly 11,000 to 34,000 Shorts
  views earn what 1,000 long-form views earn. Shorts are a discovery funnel for the
  channel, not the revenue engine. The long video is the product.

### YouTube Shorts
- Accepted: vertical or square videos up to 3 minutes can be categorised as Shorts.
- Working range, 2026 guides: 30-45 seconds is the default for educational and
  storytelling Shorts; 20-60 seconds for one idea; up to 3 minutes only when the story
  genuinely needs the context. Shorts under 20 seconds rarely convert viewers into
  subscribers (not enough time to show why to follow); Shorts over 60 seconds compete
  with the long-form library and viewers drop off because the value density feels low.
- Music trap: a Short over 1 minute with an active Content ID claim can be blocked
  globally, and most Shorts Audio Library tracks are licensed only up to 90 seconds
  in a 3 minute Short (some only 60 or 30). Our vertical cuts must use music from our
  own cleared library only, never a platform audio library track, so this trap does
  not apply to us.
- Tension with BRAND: our vertical is 60-90 seconds by BRAND section 6, which is
  longer than the Shorts sweet spot (30-45 seconds) but inside the narrative band
  (60-90 seconds for a story with 3+ beats). Resolution: cut the vertical at 60-75
  seconds where the story allows, keep the loop-back ending so a 70 second cut still
  replays, and never stretch a 45 second idea to 90 seconds to fill the BRAND range.

### TikTok
- Accepted: up to 10 minutes, but short-form means under 60 seconds in practice, and
  the creation screen's duration choices vary by account, region, and rollout.
- Working range, 2026: educational 30-45 seconds is the sweet spot (completion rate
  is the top metric; TikTok's own creator data put 21-34 seconds highest for
  completion, and narrative content holds 35-50 seconds). Storytelling and vlogs run
  60-90 seconds when tightly edited with no wasted seconds. Under 15 seconds loops
  well but the algorithm deprioritised pure looping in 2026 in favour of genuine
  engagement signals.
- The 3 second hook rule is non-negotiable on TikTok regardless of total length. Our
  vertical hook (lever plus question plus one visual shock in 3 seconds) already meets
  it; do not add a title card before the hook.
- Our 60-90 second vertical sits at the top of TikTok's narrative band. It works only
  with the BRAND structure intact: own hook, one rule-setting line, 3 escalation steps,
  twist, ticket, loop-back line. If a cut cannot hold attention past 45 seconds in
  review, cut it to 45 seconds rather than shipping a 75 second version that dies
  halfway.

### Instagram Reels
- Accepted: creators can record and edit Reels up to 20 minutes, but Instagram says
  Reels over 3 minutes will not be recommended to new audiences. That is a hard
  practical cap: our vertical must stay under 3 minutes, and our 60-90 seconds is
  inside the education band (30-90 seconds).
- Working range: 15-45 seconds for fast discovery, 30-90 seconds for education, up to
  3 minutes only when context improves the result.
- Ranking signal, 2026: per Head of Instagram Adam Mosseri, "sends per reach" (how
  often people DM a post to a friend) is the number one ranking signal, especially in
  Reels and Discover, and sends are driven by early viewers. Practical consequence:
  our verticals and captions should end on something people send to a friend, which
  is exactly what the pinned comment question ("what should the machine run next?")
  and the guess-before-the-twist already do. Do not add engagement bait; a genuine
  question is the mechanism, bait is a penalty risk.

### Facebook Reels
- Same Meta safe zones and AI labelling as Instagram Reels (section 2 and 4).
- View counting differs and must never be compared across platforms: Facebook counts
  a view at 3 seconds, YouTube at 30 seconds. A Facebook view is not a YouTube view.
- 44% of Facebook video views are muted (2026 measurement, from the channel's earlier
  research). Captions are mandatory on Facebook cuts, not optional. Our burned-in
  subtitles already cover this, provided they sit inside the safe zone.
- Monetization status (checked 3 Nov 2026): the old programs (in-stream ads, Ads on
  Reels, Performance bonus) ended 31 Aug 2025. The only program now is Facebook
  Content Monetization, which is invite-only, and Vietnam is not on Meta's official
  Content Monetization country list. Paths open from Vietnam: Stars (500 followers
  held 30 days) and Subscriptions (5,000 followers; Vietnam is on that list). Treat
  Facebook as a discovery funnel for the YouTube channel until eligibility changes;
  do not build revenue plans on Facebook ads.

## 2. Safe zones (1080x1920 vertical frame)

No platform publishes an official safe zone for ORGANIC posts; every published figure
is scoped to ads. The numbers below are the ads figures used as design margins, which
is the safe direction to err in (organic posts have no call-to-action button and no
"Sponsored" label, so their real margins are slightly smaller).

- Meta unified Reels and Stories safe zone (March 2026): 14% top, 35% bottom, 6% sides.
  On a 1080x1920 frame that is about 269 px top, 672 px bottom, 65 px sides. The 35%
  bottom is the big one: caption, follow button, and audio label stack there.
- TikTok: about 130 px top, about 484 px bottom, about 140 px right for the action
  column, plus an "Add to Playlist" button added January 2026 widening the right dead
  zone by about 20 px.
- YouTube Shorts: about 380 px top (title, channel) and about 380 px bottom
  (comments, likes, subscribe), 60 px left, 120 px right.
- Unified cross-platform safe rectangle (worst case from each side, the only rect a
  single render can trust on all three): x from 60 to 900 px, y from 270 to 1250 px,
  that is an 840 x 980 px box. Right margin 180 px covers TikTok's engagement column
  plus playlist button. Side margins bind hardest beside the button column, roughly
  y 45-65% of frame height; text above the column may use more width.
- Subtitle band: y 900-1250 px (47-65% of frame height), block centre about y 1000 px
  (52%). Nothing important below y 1250 px (65%) on Reels. This is the single most
  common vertical mistake: bottom-anchored subtitles get eaten by the caption overlay.
- BRAND conflict, flagged: BRAND section 4 says subtitles sit in a "fixed bottom
  zone". For 16:9 long-form that is correct. For 9:16 vertical, "bottom zone" must
  mean the 47-65% band above, NOT the literal bottom of the frame. The engine's
  vertical subtitle position must use the band, or the words will sit under TikTok's
  and Reels' UI on every post.
- Practical rule: build every 9:16 video at 1080x1920, keep all critical content
  (text, logo, ticket, sim clock, calls to action) inside the unified rectangle, keep
  corner marks out of the right rail (top-left for the IF lever tag, per BRAND
  section 6 thumbnails), and check visually in each app before the first publish of
  a new template.

## 3. Captions and hashtags

- Instagram hashtag cap, hard rule since 18 Dec 2025: at most 5 hashtags per post or
  Reel. Instagram's @creators account announced it, and Instagram's Help Center says
  a comment with more than 5 tags will not post. The old 30 tag limit is gone, and
  any saved 30 tag block must be deleted. Instagram's own advice: fewer, more
  targeted tags instead of many generic ones. Mosseri: hashtags do not increase reach;
  they label the post for search, and keywords belong in the caption, not the comments.
- BRAND conflict, flagged: BRAND section 7 sets fixed hashtags #whatif and
  #theifmachine plus 2-4 topic hashtags, which totals up to 6. On Instagram that
  exceeds the cap. Rule for Instagram only: fixed 2 plus at most 3 topic tags, total
  5. On TikTok, YouTube, and Facebook, the BRAND count (2 fixed plus 2-4 topic) still
  applies, but never stuff: if 3 accurate tags describe the post, stop at 3.
- Caption practice that holds everywhere in 2026: write the caption in the words
  people search for (the question itself, in plain language), put the hook in the
  first line (everything after the fold is hidden), keep the AI disclosure line on
  every video (BRAND section 7, mandatory), and put hashtags at the end of the
  caption, not in a first comment. The first-comment hashtag trick is dead: Instagram
  reads caption and comment tags identically for search, and comments can be deleted
  by bugs.
- TikTok caption practice: short, plain, question-led, 3-5 tags maximum in practice
  (no hard cap published, but tag stuffing reads as spam and the For You feed
  penalises unoriginal content, not tag counts). The video's first frame and first
  spoken line do the discovery work, not the caption.
- YouTube description practice: the BRAND template already covers it (question, run
  number, 2-3 hook sentences without spoiling the twist, sources list, guesses
  disclaimer, AI narration line, hashtags). Note YouTube shows the AI label in the
  expanded description for disclosed content, so the description is also where the
  platform's own label lives. Keep the first 2 lines clean: they are what search
  shows.

## 4. AI labelling: what each platform requires, and what it means for us

The test on every platform is REALISM, not the tool. Clearly unrealistic content,
such as animation, and AI help with scripts, captions, or colour grading, generally
needs no label anywhere. A realistic person, place, event, or voice made or changed
by AI needs one everywhere. All three platforms will also apply a label themselves
from C2PA Content Credentials or their own detection, and a platform-applied label
generally cannot be removed by the creator.

### YouTube
- Rule: disclose in YouTube Studio (Attributes section, "AI use" question, formerly
  "Altered or synthetic content") when the video contains realistic content a viewer
  could mistake for a real person, place, scene, or event: a real person appearing to
  say or do something they did not, altered footage of a real event or place, a
  realistic scene that never happened, or AI music carrying the video.
- Exempt, per YouTube's own page: clearly unrealistic content such as an animated
  world, minor edits, filters, colour adjustment, AI-written scripts, and cloning
  your own voice for a voiceover.
- Auto-labelling: since 27 May 2026 YouTube labels realistic AI itself when it detects
  it (own tools, C2PA credentials, internal detection), even without creator
  disclosure. Labels sit below the player on long-form and overlay on Shorts.
- Effect: YouTube states disclosure does not limit audience or monetization
  eligibility. Skipping a required disclosure risks a manually applied label that
  cannot be removed, removal, or suspension from the Partner Program.
- What it means for us: our videos are clearly animated cartoon worlds, so the
  mandatory trigger does not fire on the visuals. Our narration is synthetic, but it
  is not a real person's cloned voice, and it is not presented as a real person. We
  still carry the BRAND AI narration line ("Narration is AI-generated. Animation is
  illustrative, not real footage.") on EVERY video and description, which is stricter
  than the platform minimum and future-proofs us if a platform reclassifies synthetic
  narration. Do not toggle the Studio AI question to Yes for a purely animated video
  unless the episode contains a realistic element; a wrong Yes is also a mislabel.
  Recheck this at first upload: the Studio wording changed twice in two years.

### TikTok
- Rule: the Community Guidelines (version in force since 24 Sep 2026) require the
  "AI-generated content" label, or a clear caption, sticker, or watermark, on
  AI-generated or significantly edited content showing realistic-looking scenes or
  people. Toggle it in post settings under More options, before publishing; it
  cannot be removed after posting.
- Auto-labelling: TikTok reads C2PA Content Credentials and applies the label
  automatically, plus invisible watermarks and detection models. Once an auto label
  is on, the creator cannot remove it. TikTok reports over 1.3 billion videos labelled
  this way. Unlabelled realistic AI content may be removed, restricted, or labelled
  by TikTok.
- Exempt: clearly unrealistic or animated content, beauty filters, background blur,
  AI help with scripts, titles, captions, and hashtags, upscaling, and cloning your
  own voice for dubs.
- Effect: TikTok states the label itself does not reduce distribution, provided the
  content is not violative and meets For You eligibility. What costs distribution is
  unoriginal content: TikTok's Unoriginal Content rule makes reused material without
  anything new ineligible for the For You feed.
- What it means for us: same position as YouTube. Our animation is exempt by the
  stated rule, but our files must not carry stray C2PA manifests from AI tools that
  would trigger an auto label we cannot remove, and we disclose in the caption
  anyway per BRAND. Check the export pipeline: if a tool in the chain writes Content
  Credentials into the MP4, decide deliberately whether to keep them (honest, but
  the label sticks) or strip them (cleaner, but then our own caption line is the only
  disclosure, which BRAND already requires).

### Meta (Instagram, Facebook, Threads)
- Rule: use the "Add AI label" toggle (label shown as "AI info", formerly "Made with
  AI") when posting organic content with photorealistic video or realistic-sounding
  audio that was digitally created or altered, including with AI. Meta may apply
  penalties for skipping a required disclosure. For content that was only AI-edited,
  the label may sit in the post's three-dot menu rather than on the post itself.
- Exempt, stated: AI images do not require the label (though Meta may label them
  itself), and an unmistakably animated result is outside the stated photorealistic
  video threshold solely because AI was used. A cartoon-style landscape video does
  not need one, per Meta's own examples.
- Caution, specific to us: Meta's own examples of content that DOES need a label
  include "a reel narrated with a realistic AI voiceover". Our Operator voice is
  designed to sound warm and human, which is close enough to "realistic-sounding
  audio" that a cautious reading triggers the toggle. Combined with the June 2026
  update (Meta detects ads made or edited with third-party AI tools and adds an AI
  info label itself), the safe rule for Meta surfaces is: turn the toggle ON for
  every vertical cut, and keep the BRAND caption disclosure line. A label is
  informational, not a ranking penalty, on Meta's own account; unoriginal content is
  the penalty (accounts repeatedly posting unoriginal photos and carousels lost
  recommendations entirely in Meta's April 2026 enforcement).
- EU layer: the EU AI Act's transparency duties for realistic AI content phased in
  from August 2026. Our audience is US/UK/CA/AU, but a UK or EU viewer seeing a
  realistic-sounding voice should still get the disclosure. The BRAND line covers it.

### The monetization rule behind the labels (read this before worrying about labels)
- YouTube's inauthentic content policy (renamed from "repetitious content" 15 Jul
  2025, clarified 16 Jul 2026 into three named buckets: Generic or Repetitive
  Content, Unsatisfying or Off-putting Content, AI Personas Related to Sensitive
  Topics) blocks monetization for content that "looks like it's made with a
  template" or "may feel repetitive to viewers after watching several videos in a
  row", including "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with
  minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" and "AI-generated
  content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass
  production". The test YouTube sets is that "the substance of each video should be
  materially varied". The policy judges what is on the channel, not which tools made
  it, and its own allowed examples include using AI to visualise a story you
  invented.
- What it means for us, concretely: the machine rituals (lever, clock, ticket) are a
  FORMAT, not a template, only while the substance varies. Every episode needs its
  own question, its own world, its own escalation, its own twist, and its own answer.
  Shipping the same structure with nouns swapped, or shipping slideshow-style
  verticals with minimal narration, is exactly the failure mode. The research packs,
  the per-episode world design, and the fixed single narrator are the defence, and
  they only work if episode substance genuinely differs. This is an engine design
  constraint, not a legal footnote.

## 5. Posting times for a US/UK audience (starting defaults, replace with analytics)

General law, 2026: the first 60 minutes after publishing account for roughly 70% of
a post's eventual reach in US samples, because early engagement velocity is a ranking
signal on every platform. Post 30-60 minutes BEFORE the audience peak, not on it, and
reply to comments in the first hour.

- YouTube long-form: 6-10pm Eastern is the US evening peak, with a smaller weekday
  window at 7-9am. Overlap slot that also serves the UK evening: 3-4pm Eastern, which
  is 8-9pm in the UK and mid-afternoon on the US East Coast. Recommended default for
  our Tue/Thu/Sat long videos: 3pm Eastern (8pm UK), moving to 7pm Eastern once US
  viewers dominate the analytics.
- YouTube Shorts: Tuesday-Thursday, 2-6pm Eastern, with Tuesday 4pm and Wednesday 3pm
  the strongest single slots in Buffer's 2026 analysis. Weekend peak shifts earlier,
  9am-3pm Eastern.
- TikTok: Tuesday 9am, Thursday 12pm, Friday 5pm Eastern are the strongest single
  slots; broader windows are weekday mornings 7-9am, lunch 12-2pm, and late evening
  9-11pm Eastern. TikTok is less time-sensitive than YouTube, but peak-hour posting
  still gives a stronger initial push.
- Instagram Reels: Monday-Friday, 9am-12pm Eastern, with Wednesday 11am the top single
  slot. Secondary window 7-10pm Eastern.
- Facebook: 8am-12pm Eastern (Buffer 2026 benchmark).
- Scheduling reality: a 3pm Eastern publish is 3am in Vietnam and 8pm in London.
  Everything must be scheduled, not posted live, and the owner approves episodes in
  advance for exactly this reason. Never post at Vietnam daytime and call it a US
  strategy.

## 6. Conflicts between BRAND defaults and platform rules (resolved here)

1. Instagram hashtags: BRAND allows 2 fixed plus 2-4 topic (up to 6). Instagram caps
   at 5. On Instagram: 2 fixed plus at most 3 topic.
2. Vertical subtitles: BRAND says "fixed bottom zone". On vertical, that zone is the
   47-65% band (y 900-1250 px on 1080x1920), not the literal bottom.
3. Vertical length: BRAND says 60-90 seconds. Shorts sweet spot is 30-45 seconds,
   TikTok narrative band is 60-90 seconds, Reels education band is 30-90 seconds.
   Cut at 60-75 seconds where the story allows; never stretch to fill 90.
4. AI label toggles: BRAND requires the caption disclosure line everywhere. On Meta
   surfaces, also turn the platform toggle ON for every vertical cut, because Meta's
   examples include realistic AI voiceover narration. On YouTube and TikTok, leave
   the platform toggle to the realism test (animation is exempt), but keep the
   caption line always.
5. Thumbnail tag position: BRAND puts the yellow IF lever tag top-left. Safe zones
   agree: top-left is clear on all three vertical platforms (the danger column is on
   the right). No conflict, keep it.
