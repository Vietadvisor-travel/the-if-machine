# Research pack — drifting-continents · "How do we know the continents move?" · FATHOM · Claude, 17 Nov 2026

## The question
The ground under our feet feels like the one thing that never moves. Yet South America and Africa fit together like torn paper. How did we find out that whole continents drift — when the movement is far too slow to see?

## Facts
| # | fact | sources |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | The coastlines of South America and Africa match — noticed on maps since the 1500s–1600s | S1, S2 |
| F2 | 1912 (lecture), 1915 (book *Die Entstehung der Kontinente und Ozeane*): Alfred Wegener, a meteorologist, proposed that the continents drift | S1, S3 |
| F3 | Fossils: Mesosaurus, a small freshwater reptile, only in southern Africa and South America; the seed fern Glossopteris in South America, Africa, India, Australia — and Antarctica | S2, S4 |
| F4 | Geologists rejected it: no force could plough continents through the ocean floor | S2, S3 |
| F5 | Wegener died on the Greenland ice cap in November 1930, during an expedition, decades before he was proved right (verify second source) | S3, (S5) |
| F6 | 1950s–60s: ships towing magnetometers found STRIPES of magnetism on the sea floor, parallel and symmetrical on both sides of the mid-ocean ridges | S6, S7 |
| F7 | Sept 1963, Nature: Vine and Matthews (and Morley independently): new sea floor is made at the ridge and records the Earth's magnetic flips — a tape recorder of the field: the sea floor spreads | S6, S7, S8 |
| F8 | Today the Atlantic widens by a few centimetres a year (≈ 2.5–4 cm; about as fast as fingernails grow — verify) | S9, S10 |

## Clues
1. **The torn map** (F1, F2). 2. **The same fossils on both sides** (F3). 3. **The mocked idea and the man on the ice** (F4, F5). 4. **The striped sea floor** (F6, F7).
Twist: the proof was written on the bottom of the ocean in bands of magnetism, like a tape — Wegener never saw it.
