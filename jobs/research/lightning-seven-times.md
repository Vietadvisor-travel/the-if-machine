## Key numbers (exact, with unit and source; "about" where the sources disagree)

- Strikes: 7, between 1942 and 1977 (Guinness; Washington Post).
- Strike dates: Apr 1942, Jul 1969, Jul 1970, Apr 1972, Aug 1973, Jun 1976, 25 Jun 1977 (Guinness for the months; the 25 June date for the seventh is from the Washington Post account).
- 7th strike: 25 June 1977 (Washington Post); Guinness gives June 1977.
- Odds: 4.15 in 100 million trillion trillion (GWU statistics professor, via Washington Post). This assumes pure chance. Do NOT present it as a measured fact; he worked outdoors in storms for 36 years.
- One strike in an 80-year lifespan: around 1 in 10,000 (Guinness).
- Died: 28 Sep 1983, aged 71 (Washington Post; Guinness confirms 1983, aged 71).

## Sensitivity (victims, families, living people, contested points) and how to tell it with respect

- He died by suicide on 28 Sep 1983. Tell with respect, end on the record, never the punchline. Do not show or describe the death; the clip ends on the seventh strike and the record.
- Say "recorded as" for the seven strikes: the Washington Post 2013 investigation found no witness saw all seven and the Park Service file is incomplete. Keep the Guinness recognition; do not present the count as beyond doubt.
- Do not present the odds number as a measured fact. It assumes each strike is pure chance, and he worked outdoors in storms for 36 years.
- His wife was also struck once; mention it only if it fits the 60 seconds, and with the same respect.
