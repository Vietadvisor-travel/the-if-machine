## 1. What if Earth stopped spinning, for good?

- Vietnamese name: Trai Dat ngung quay, mai mai
- Slug: earth-stops-spinning
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: the 5-second version is the famous one, but a permanent stop is a different
  planet: the oceans slide to the poles, one day lasts a year, and the magnetic field
  dies with the spin.
- Facts:
  1. The ground at the equator moves at about 1,670 km/h, so a sudden stop would throw
     the air, oceans and everything loose eastward at that speed. (What If the Earth
     Suddenly Stopped Spinning? | IFLScience, Jan 2018,
     https://www.iflscience.com/what-if-the-earth-abruptly-stopped-spinning-74983)
  2. In a slow stop, Esri's Witold Fraczek modeled the oceans migrating to the poles,
     leaving two polar oceans and one giant equatorial continent, with ocean depth
     dropping about 8 km at the equator. (Same IFLScience article, Jan 2018)
  3. With no spin, one full day-night cycle would take about a year: roughly six
     months of daylight then six months of dark, heating the day side past 100 C.
     (The thought experiment: What would happen if the Earth stopped spinning? | BBC
     Science Focus Magazine, accessed 1 Nov 2026, via
     https://www.pippasworld.com/what-if/what-if-the-earth-stopped-spinning/)
  4. A non-rotating Earth would lose its magnetic field, because the liquid core
     dynamo depends on rotation, leaving survivors exposed to radiation. (Same
     IFLScience article, Jan 2018)
  5. Earth already slows by about 1.7 milliseconds per century; at that rate a day as
     long as a year would take 18.5 billion years. (Same IFLScience article, Jan 2018)
- Score: 86 (curiosity 36, drawable 18, gap 14, verifiable 18)
- Gap note: What If did "Earth Stopped Spinning for 5 Seconds" (6.72M views, Dec 2021,
  under the 10M bar). Ours is the permanent stop, a different question with a different
  ending.
- Why now: evergreen; the slowdown fact (1.7 ms per century) gives a fresh hook, and
  the week opens on Carl Sagan Day, 9 Nov 2026, a natural space peg.
- Keywords: what if earth stopped spinning, earth stops rotating, what if earth stopped

  1. What If Earth Stopped Spinning Forever? (39)

## 3. What if the ocean was fresh water?

- Vietnamese name: Dai duong hoa nuoc ngot
- Slug: ocean-fresh-water
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: the ocean is not salty by accident; salt is the engine. Take it away and the
  global conveyor belt stops, Europe freezes, and the sea goes quiet.
- Facts:
  1. The open ocean averages about 35 parts per thousand of salt, about 35 grams per
     kilogram of water. (How Salt Steers the Ocean | Ocean Ambassadors,
     https://oceanambassadors.org/how-salt-steers-the-ocean/)
  2. Salt plus temperature sets seawater density, and density drives the thermohaline
     circulation, a global conveyor that takes water roughly a thousand years to
     complete one loop. (Same Ocean Ambassadors article)
  3. Its warm surface limb, including the Gulf Stream, carries tropical heat north and
     is a large part of why north-west Europe has mild winters at the latitude of
     Labrador. (Same Ocean Ambassadors article)
  4. Sea ice forms almost fresh, only about 5 parts per thousand, and seawater at
     35 parts per thousand freezes at about minus 1.9 C. (Same Ocean Ambassadors
     article)
  5. More fresh water makes surface water less dense so it sinks less, slowing the
     conveyor that moves heat around the planet. (Study Game: The Hydrosphere |
     StudyFetch, https://www.studyfetch.com/games/play/6a04f9fa9bebfba78ea6e7cf)
- Score: 82 (curiosity 33, drawable 16, gap 16, verifiable 17)
- Gap note: no big what-if channel has a "fresh water ocean" episode found in this
  scan; ice-melt videos cover the reverse direction.
- Why now: evergreen; World Science Day for Peace and Development is 10 Nov 2026, a
  clean peg for an ocean-climate explainer.
- Keywords: what if ocean was freshwater, ocean salinity, thermohaline circulation
- Titles (max 55 chars):

  2. No More Salt Water: The Ocean Stops Moving (42)

## 4. What if humans hibernated like bears?

- Vietnamese name: Con nguoi ngu dong nhu gau
- Slug: humans-hibernate
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: a bear sleeps 5 to 7 months without eating, drinking or peeing, and wakes up
  with its muscles intact. A human doing the same would die of clots, bone loss and
  muscle waste in weeks. The bear, not the human, is the better astronaut.
- Facts:
  1. A black bear settles in its den in November and does not eat, drink, urinate or
     defecate until spring, 5 to 7 months on body fat alone, heart 8 to 10 beats per
     minute, losing up to a third of its body weight yet keeping its muscles. (Why do
     some animals sleep for months but humans cannot? | TrenderHQ, 2 Nov 2026,
     https://trenderhq.com/why-do-some-animals-sleep-for-months-hibernation-but-humans-cannot/)
  2. Bears recycle urea from their own waste back into protein, which is how they lose
     almost no muscle despite eating nothing, and they recycle calcium so they lose no
     bone mass. (Same TrenderHQ article, 2 Nov 2026)
  3. ESA calls the bear the best role model for human hibernation in space: similar
     body mass to us, body temperature down only a few degrees, a limit considered
     safe for humans. (Bear sleeping | ESA,
     https://www.esa.int/media/bear-sleeping)
  4. NASA's NIAC torpor habitat study proposes putting Mars crews into stasis for the
     trip, and NASA's STASH program aims to fly hibernating ground squirrels on the
     ISS within a few years. (Torpor Inducing Transfer Habitat for Human Stasis to
     Mars | NASA NIAC, 11 May 2014,
     https://www.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/files/Bradford_2013_PhI_Torpor.pdf;
     Could Humans Hibernate Their Way to Mars? | 1Ban News, 14 Jul 2026,
     https://1ban.news/human-hibernation-mars-synthetic-torpor/)
  5. In 2023 researchers induced torpor in rats, which are not natural hibernators,
     with focused ultrasound: body temperature down about 3 C, heart rate down 47
     percent, metabolism shifted to burning fat only. (Same 1Ban News article,
     14 Jul 2026)

- Gap note: no big what-if channel has a human-hibernation episode in this scan;

## 5. What if you could see in the dark?

- Vietnamese name: Nhin xuyen bong toi
- Slug: see-in-the-dark
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: true night vision is not brighter eyes, it is seeing heat. The world would
  turn into a thermal map, and color would be gone.
- Facts:
  1. Cats need only about one sixth of the ambient light human eyes need, and can see
     roughly six to seven times better than us in dim light. (Can Cats See in the
     Dark? | Purina Canada, https://www.purina.ca/articles/cat/behaviour/can-cats-see-in-the-dark;
     What the Predator Sees | Biology Insights,
     https://biologyinsights.com/what-the-predator-sees-heat-uv-and-darkness/)
  2. The tapetum lucidum, a reflective layer behind the retina, bounces light back for
     a second pass across the photoreceptors, effectively doubling photon capture.
     (Same Biology Insights article)
  3. Cats have about six times more rod cells than humans, and far fewer cones, so
     they see better in dim light but worse in bright daylight and see red only as
     shades of gray. (Cat Eyes, Pet Tip 93 | Pets.ca,
     https://www.pets.ca/cats/tips/cat-eyes-pet-tip-93/)
  4. Pit vipers detect temperature changes as small as 0.003 C and pick up a mouse's
     body heat from over 60 cm away, seeing warm shapes against a cooler background
     in complete darkness. (Same Biology Insights article)
  5. Humans lack a tapetum lucidum entirely and rely on rods packed with rhodopsin
     for low-light vision. (Understanding Night Vision | Raven Rock Armory,
     https://ravenrockarmory.com/articles/understanding-night-vision-how-we-see-in-the-dark/)
- Score: 81 (curiosity 32, drawable 17, gap 14, verifiable 18)
- Gap note: night-vision explainers are common, but no big what-if channel frames it
  as "you wake up with night vision tomorrow"; Curiosity Hub's adjacent episode is
  about breathing in space, not seeing in the dark.
- Why now: evergreen; nights lengthen into winter in the northern hemisphere, and the
  week opens on World Science Day, 10 Nov 2026.
- Keywords: what if you could see in the dark, night vision, tapetum lucidum
- Titles (max 55 chars):
  1. What If You Could See in the Dark? (34)
  2. Night Vision Eyes: What Would You See? (38)
  3. Seeing Heat: The World After Dark (33)
- Risk: none serious; be clear that cat night vision still needs some light, it is
  not seeing in total darkness.

## 6. What if you could breathe underwater?

- Vietnamese name: Tho duoc duoi nuoc
- Slug: breathe-underwater
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: gills would not save you. The real limit is not oxygen, it is carbon
  dioxide, cold and pressure: your urge to breathe comes from CO2 buildup, not from
  running out of air.
- Facts:
  1. The official static apnea world record is 11 minutes 35 seconds, held by French
     freediver Stephane Mifsud since 2009; with pure oxygen beforehand, Budimir Sobat
     reached 24 minutes 37 seconds in 2021. (The Science of Freediving | Organizer
     Tone, https://organizertone.com/en/the-science-of-freediving-how-humans-hold-their-breath-for-10-minutes-1825924/?hl=en&s1=69b198b1880999780f01b950;
     Has any human breathe underwater? | Calendar UK,
     https://www.calendar-uk.co.uk/frequently-asked-questions/has-any-human-breathe-underwater)
  2. The mammalian dive reflex, shared by humans, drops heart rate to as low as 10
     to 15 beats per minute in elite freedivers and pushes blood to the brain and
     heart. (Same Organizer Tone article)
  3. Fish gills extract around 75 percent of the oxygen that passes through them,
     while the oxygen concentration in water is far lower than in air. (Beyond the
     Gasp | Oreate AI,
     https://www.oreateai.com/blog/beyond-the-gasp-mastering-the-art-of-breathing-underwater/043381eadd8d483994ed07c2c1b8285e)
  4. The urge to breathe is triggered by carbon dioxide buildup in the blood, not by
     lack of oxygen; the brain cannot measure blood oxygen directly. (Same Organizer
     Tone article)
  5. One estimate puts a human's oxygen need at the amount dissolved in about 12
     cubic meters of seawater per second, far more water than any gills could pump.
     (Same Calendar UK page; treat as an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precise
     figure)
- Score: 81 (curiosity 33, drawable 17, gap 13, verifiable 18)
- Gap note: Curiosity Hub did "What If You Could Breathe in Space?" (Oct 2026); ours
  is the underwater version, gills and pressure instead of vacuum, a different
  physics problem.
- Why now: evergreen; the freediving records and the dive reflex give it a clean
  human-body hook with no date pressure.
- Keywords: what if you could breathe underwater, human gills, freediving record
- Titles (max 55 chars):
  1. What If You Could Breathe Underwater? (37)
  2. Gills for Humans: Why They Would Fail (37)
  3. Breathe Underwater: The Cold Truth (34)

  "about" and lean on the gill efficiency and CO2 facts instead.

## 7. What if humans were only 10 cm tall?

- Vietnamese name: Con nguoi cao co 10 cm
- Slug: humans-10cm
- Type: evergreen
- Twist: tiny humans would be ant-strong and could jump over houses, but they would
  have to eat all day and would freeze fast, because size is physics, not a costume.
- Facts:
  1. The square-cube law, published by Galileo in 1638: double a creature's height
     and its surface area grows 4 times while its weight grows 8 times, so strength
     scales with area and weight with volume. (Galileo's Square-Cube Law | Dinosaur
     Theory, https://dinosaurtheory.com/scaling.html)
  2. A leafcutter ant carries objects 50 times its body weight; a human lifts roughly
     its own weight; an Asian elephant only about 25 percent of its own weight.
     (Same Dinosaur Theory page)
  3. The Etruscan shrew, one of the smallest mammals, eats up to two times its body
     weight every day and its heart beats about 1,500 times per minute, because it
     loses heat so fast. (The Weird Reality of Creatures Big and Small |
     Manual do Bruxo,
     https://manualdobruxo.com.br/the-weird-reality-of-creatures-big-and-small-and-why-sizes-change-1pji)
  4. Small warm-blooded animals must eat constantly to replace lost heat; the
     Etruscan shrew at 2 grams must eat many times its body weight each day to
     survive. (Scaling and Order-of-Magnitude Estimates | Central Oregon Community
     College Physics,
     http://coccweb.cocc.edu/bemerson/public_html/physics/ZGlobalResources/CCTextAlgebra/ATNewtonian/Chapters/CCAT1Estimates.html)
  5. Fleas jump about 200 times their own height, which only works because they are
     tiny. (The Square-Cube Law | Tropedia,
     https://tropedia.fandom.com/wiki/Square-Cube_Law)
- Score: 87 (curiosity 35, drawable 18, gap 17, verifiable 17)
- Gap note: no big what-if channel has a "10 cm humans" episode in this scan; scale

- Why now: evergreen; no date pressure, and it is the most drawable candidate in the

## 9. What if you never had to eat?

- Vietnamese name: Khong bao gio phai an

- Type: evergreen
- Twist: never needing food would not set you free. Your gut bacteria would starve,

     https://joesfrenchitalian.com/how-long-can-you-go-without-food-survival-effects-and-more/)

     https://biologyinsights.com/what-happens-if-you-starve-yourself-for-a-week/)
  3. Cutting off food for a week reshapes the gut microbiome substantially: one study
     found Proteobacteria expanded roughly six-fold while other groups fell by a
     third to a half. (Same Biology Insights article)
  4. The longest medically supervised fast on record is Angus Barbieri's 382 days in
     1965, but he took vitamins, electrolytes, tea, coffee and yeast for amino
     acids. (How Long Can You Last Without Food? | CopyProgramming,
     https://copyprogramming.com/howto/how-many-days-can-you-last-without-food)
  5. Starvation weakens the immune system, mostly through extreme deficiency of
     vitamins and minerals, and people can die of immune-related disease during
     starvation. (How your body fights to keep you alive when you are starving |
