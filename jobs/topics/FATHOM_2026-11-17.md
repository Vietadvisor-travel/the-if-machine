| 1 | expanding-universe | How do we know the universe is getting bigger? | Henrietta Leavitt's star "candles" (1912) · Slipher's galaxies all redder (1912–17) · Hubble's line (1929) | a priest, Lemaître, worked it out first (1927) and was ignored | a lamp in the dark becomes a star becomes a ruler stretching across the sky; a raisin cake rising in an oven becomes the cosmos | Harvard plates, Hubble's 1929 diagram, Mount Wilson engravings |
| 2 | dna-shape | How did we see the shape of DNA when no one can see it? | Miescher's "nuclein" from bandages (1869) · Chargaff's rules · X-ray shadows (Franklin's Photo 51, 1952) · the cardboard model (1953) | the picture that cracked it was a blurred X — taken by a woman left out of the prize | a shadow of an X becomes a spiral staircase you can climb; the staircase unzips into two | 1953 Nature page, Photo 51 (check licence), lab engravings |
| 3 | sun-fire | What makes the Sun shine? | coal would burn out in 5,000 years · Kelvin: it is shrinking (only millions of years) · Einstein's E=mc² · Eddington (1920) and Bethe (1939): fusion | the proof came from particles that pass through the whole Earth — and at first a third of them were missing (solved 2001) | a lump of coal on fire becomes the Sun becomes a tiny nucleus collision; a mine full of cleaning fluid catching ghosts | Kelvin & Eddington papers, Homestake mine photos |
| 4 | invisible-killers | How did we discover that invisible germs kill? | Semmelweis and the two maternity wards (1847) · Snow's water (link to cholera-map, kept separate) · Pasteur's swan-neck flask (1859) · Koch's photographs of bacteria (1876–82) | the doctor who said "wash your hands" was laughed at and died in an asylum | a doctor's hand becomes a landscape of living specks; a broth that stays clear for 150 years | Pasteur flask engravings, Koch plates, hospital prints |
| 5 | drifting-continents | How do we know the continents move? | the jigsaw coastlines · the same fossils across oceans (Wegener 1912) · stripes of magnetism on the sea floor (1963) · GPS today: a few cm a year | Wegener was mocked for 50 years and died on the ice before he was proved right | a world map torn like paper and pushed back together; the sea floor as a tape recorder of the Earth's field | Wegener's 1915 maps, fossil plates, old world maps |
| 6 | dinosaur-killer | How do we know what killed the dinosaurs? | a thin clay line in Gubbio, Italy · too much iridium (Alvarez 1980) · shocked quartz · the buried crater at Chicxulub found from oil-survey data (1991) | the crater was hiding in plain sight, under a Mexican town, known to an oil company for a decade | a line of clay no thicker than a finger becomes the last day of a world | geological sections, 19th-c. dinosaur engravings |
| 7 | ice-ages | How do we know the Earth was once frozen? | boulders that don't belong (erratics) · Agassiz's glaciers (1837) · ice cores: bubbles of ancient air · the wobble of the orbit (Milankovitch) | the evidence that ice once covered Europe was lying in every Swiss field — scratched rocks | a boulder in a meadow slides back up a vanished glacier; a bubble in ice becomes a breath of 800,000-year-old air | Agassiz's plates (1840), alpine engravings |
| 8 | black-hole-photo | How do you photograph something that swallows light? | Michell's "dark stars" (1783) · Einstein's bent light (eclipse 1919) · stars racing round the centre of our galaxy (Nobel 2020) · a telescope the size of the Earth (2019) | the first picture took 5 petabytes of data flown on hard drives — faster than the internet | the 1919 eclipse photograph bends into the ring of light; eight telescopes become one eye the size of the planet | 1919 eclipse plates, Michell's paper, EHT image (CC BY — artefact only) |
| 9 | body-electric | How did we discover the body runs on electricity? | Galvani's twitching frog legs (1780s) · Volta's pile (1800) · Helmholtz times a nerve (1850, about 30 m/s) · the first heart trace (Einthoven 1903) | a quarrel between two Italians about a dead frog gave us both the battery and neuroscience | a frog leg on a brass hook becomes a bolt becomes a nerve pulse racing up an arm | Galvani's 1791 plates, Volta's letter plates, Einthoven's machine |
| 10 | dinosaur-colour | How can we know what colour a dinosaur was? | feathers in fossils (Archaeopteryx 1861) · tiny pigment sacs (melanosomes) under the electron microscope (2010) · matching them to living birds | we now know the colour of an animal dead for 160 million years — black and white with a ginger crest (Anchiornis) | a grey stone feather blooms into colour, cell by cell | Archaeopteryx engravings (1863), 19th-c. fossil plates |
