## Facts on screen (research pack F-numbers)
- Untrained adult 30–90 s, ~3 min with determination — F9
- Static apnea on plain air 11:35, Stéphane Mifsud, 2009 — F1 · 29:03 with pure oxygen first, Vitomir Maričić, 2025, shown on a separate track as a different record — F2
- Lung packing — F8 · 133 m on one breath, Alexey Molchanov, 2024 (≈ a forty-storey tower: 40 × ~3.3 m, an analogy) — F7
- Dive reflex: cold water on the face, heart to 10–15 bpm in elite freedivers — F3 · blood to brain and heart, spleen releases red cells — F4
- Seals and whales share the reflex, theirs is stronger (general physiology, worded qualitatively) — research outline
- Bajau spleens ~50 % larger than the Saluan's, genetic (Ilardo et al., Cell 2018) — F15
- The urge to breathe is a CO₂ alarm; the brain cannot measure blood oxygen directly — F5
- Water holds ~30× less oxygen than air; ~30× more water to move — F11 · water ~800× denser (rounded) — F12 · fish gills: blood flows opposite to the water (counter-current) — F11 source (MSU Denver)
- Kylstra, Leiden, 1961: mice breathed oxygenated salt water under pressure; CO₂ removal, not oxygen, was the limit — F13
- Liquid ventilation in very sick premature infants from 1989, still experimental — F14
- Hyperventilation flushes CO₂ without adding much oxygen (blood already 97–99 % saturated); shallow water blackout is the leading cause of breath-hold diving deaths — F6 · often near the surface on the way up as pressure falls — F10
- Safety rule said out loud: never hold your breath in water alone, a buddy close enough to reach you.
